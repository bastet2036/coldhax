# Public incident accounting

Research cutoff: **2026-08-03 11:34:11 UTC**. On-chain balances below were
queried from the public [mempool.space API](https://mempool.space/api) at that
same time. The machine-readable observations and exact classifications are in
[`evidence/public-incidents.json`](evidence/public-incidents.json).

## Bottom line

The strongest public aggregate located is Galaxy Research's **suspected**
Coldcard-linked total: **1,367.05 BTC across 4,585 source addresses** in three
waves reported through August 1. This is not a vendor loss figure and is not a
cryptographic proof that every source address was generated by the defective
path. Galaxy itself says its fingerprint identifies coordinated operators, not
the vulnerability: an owner moving coins can look the same on-chain.

Public owner/witness reports provide stronger evidence that at least some
Coldcard-origin wallets were swept without owner authorization. One owner now
publicly reports an exact **18.25245043 BTC** loss across three Coldcard wallets,
but did not publish the source addresses or transaction IDs needed to verify or
place that amount within the aggregate waves. No reviewed public
source reconstructed a victim seed and matched it to a drained address, and no
source named or established the identity of an attacker. Therefore this report
classifies the aggregate waves and individual cases as **suspected attribution
to this defect**, not conclusively attributed exploitation.

An August 3 fourth-wave alert has now been revised from its initial in-progress
figures to a **709-address / 448.73 BTC surviving core after excluding 89
multisig addresses / 20.58 BTC**. The analyst explicitly says there is still no
direct victim report confirming this wave. It remains a separate potential
wave and is not added to Galaxy's reviewed Waves 1–3 cumulative figure.

## Classification rules

These are separate questions:

1. **Affected/exposed:** Was the wallet generated on an affected device and
   firmware path?
2. **Unauthorized spend:** Did an owner identify a spend as unauthorized and
   provide enough public on-chain detail to inspect it?
3. **Attributed exploitation:** Does evidence establish that this exact entropy
   defect, rather than a migration, backup compromise, phishing, malware, or
   another cause, produced the spend?

A destination's current balance is neither the amount stolen nor a victim's
loss. Consolidation fees reduce destination balances. Later hops are not new
losses. Cumulative rows below must not be added together.

## Public owner and witness reports

### Three Coldcard wallets — 18.25245043 BTC owner report

[Jonathan Goodman reported](https://x.com/itscoachgoodman/status/2083527082223563157)
that all three of his Coldcard wallets were drained between 9:36 p.m. and 9:43
p.m. on July 29. His post states an exact **18.25245043 BTC** loss, described as
just over CAD $1.6 million, and says the Coldcard device had been kept in a
safety-deposit box and had never connected to the internet. The post does not
state the time zone, Coldcard model, generation-firmware version, source
addresses, or transaction IDs.

[Bloomberg independently interviewed Goodman](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/hackers-target-bitcoin-s-safest-hiding-place-in-ongoing-attack)
and quoted the same three-wallet, seven-minute drain account. This corroborates
the owner report but does not supply the missing on-chain identifiers.

- Affected/exposed: **owner report confirms Coldcard**, but not the model or
  generation firmware.
- Unauthorized spend: **owner report confirms**.
- Attributed exploitation: **suspected, not conclusively proved**; the owner
  attributes the loss to the Coldcard flaw, but no public computational match or
  alternative-cause analysis was supplied.
- Amount: **18.25245043 BTC owner-reported**. It is not independently on-chain
  verified and must not be added to Galaxy's cumulative total because overlap
  with the aggregate waves is unresolved.

### Mk3 testing wallet — owner report with transaction ID

[Erik reported](https://x.com/eriklocalhost/status/2083875886458171626)
that a native-SegWit wallet on a Mk3 testing device was swept on August 2. He
reported approximately **9,000 sats** and linked transaction
[`d72e2…c89a4`](https://mempool.space/tx/d72e2d8e3096440c48fdd4ed0cc56a7e784d215970413210e0b2af38528c89a4).
The transaction is confirmed in block 960668 at 2026-08-02 04:03:56 UTC. It has
902 inputs, pays a 574,200-sat fee, and sends 6,490,373,764 sats to
[`bc1q0rv…f5q6m`](https://mempool.space/address/bc1q0rvn88w08j75k4h48lf9fvhan7unjp7vjf5q6m).

- Affected/exposed: **owner report confirms Mk3**; the post does not state the
  generation firmware.
- Unauthorized spend: **owner report confirms**.
- Attributed exploitation: **suspected, not conclusively proved**.
- Amount: the post says “9k sats.” It does not identify which of 902 inputs was
  the owner's, so the report does not manufacture false satoshi precision.
- Double counting: those sats are a subset of the transaction's 64.90947964 BTC
  input total, not an amount to add to that consolidation.

[Marius Offchain separately reported](https://x.com/mariusoffchain/status/2083814011859030252)
a friend's Mk2 wallet in the same consolidation. This is secondhand and does not
identify the friend's input or loss. The 64.90373764 BTC output must not be
assigned to that one person.

### Mk3-origin duress wallet — corrected third-party report

[Tomer Strolight corrected his initial report](https://x.com/TomerStrolight/status/2083578868191957292):
the deliberately funded “duress” seed originated on Mk3 and was later imported
to Mk4. It was among transactions to
[`bc1qzm5…34mf3`](https://mempool.space/address/bc1qzm5pauxyv7t7vqstzpumqcn066wfjsmev34mf3).

- The correction supports **Mk3-origin exposure**, not Mk4 seed-generation
  exploitation.
- The destination received 16 transactions totaling 0.33203236 BTC. The post
  does not identify the acquaintance's source input or amount, so that aggregate
  is not presented as the individual's loss.
- Attribution remains suspected because no public seed-to-address
  reconstruction or alternative-cause exclusion was published.

### July 31 owner report — amount deliberately unresolved

[Evan Schoenberg reported](https://x.com/evands/status/2083505832587587945)
that his wallet was drained with several approximately 0.1 BTC wallets and cited
[`bc1q0mh…sc279`](https://mempool.space/address/bc1q0mh6rs0mjvv5ncdyqwhqma7hgup3aycucsc279).
That address received 0.50986038 BTC and now has zero balance, but it is a
multi-wallet consolidation address. The public post does not establish the
firmware used to generate Evan's wallet or isolate his input, so neither the
consolidation total nor a guessed 0.1 BTC is recorded as his exact loss.

## Potential Wave 4 — not included in the verified cumulative total

At 2026-08-03 00:51 UTC, Galaxy's Alex Thorn
[reported a *likely* fourth wave](https://x.com/intangiblecoins/status/2084079706320646300)
then still in progress: 218 confirmed transactions, 462 source addresses, 216
fresh destinations, and 388.92748828 BTC in blocks 960778–960792. The blocks and
transactions exist, but this review did not independently reproduce the entire
address-set classifier or establish authorization for every spend. It is
therefore preserved as a **potential wave** and is **not added** to the 1,367.05
BTC Galaxy cumulative total.

At 03:22 UTC Thorn
[published a correction](https://x.com/intangiblecoins/status/2084117621864046698)
after Nunchuk identified multisig transactions in the confirmed-plus-mempool
pastebins. The update states: 857 addresses / 486.11 BTC as circulated; remove
89 addresses / 20.58 BTC for multisig; surviving core 709 addresses / 448.73
BTC. Those address counts and amounts do not arithmetically reconcile by simple
subtraction, so this report preserves the stated surviving-core values rather
than inventing a derivation. Thorn also states that Waves 1–3 contain zero
multisigs and that no direct victim report had yet confirmed Wave 4.

The community tracker separately subtracts six destinations with prior history
and publishes a derived 703-address / 443.34 BTC figure. Because that step
assumes those six do not overlap the 89 multisig exclusions, it is recorded as
a secondary reconstruction rather than replacing Thorn's primary stated core.

## Search scope and negative results

This review searched current Coinkite and Block publications; Galaxy Research,
owner, and analyst posts (including live RSS mirrors and X's public syndication
endpoint); public GitHub issue/repository search; the community tracker and a
new 221-destination ledger pull request; Bloomberg's new owner interview;
new CoinDesk and Decrypt Wave 4 coverage; Hacker News; Google/Bing-indexed
reporting; and public explorer data. The new coverage restates Thorn's corrected
709-address / 448.73 BTC potential Wave 4 rather than adding a direct victim or
primary transaction corpus. Reddit's public search endpoint was
inaccessible (HTTP 403). No reviewed primary source provided:

- an official Coinkite loss total or complete victim list;
- a machine-readable primary-source list of all 4,585 source addresses and every
  theft transaction;
- a public seed reconstruction tied to a drained address;
- a named-attacker or law-enforcement attribution; or
- enough detail to assign exact victim amounts in the Tomer, Evan, or Marius
  reports; or
- source addresses or transaction IDs to independently verify Goodman's exact
  owner-reported amount.

This negative result does not mean no further cases exist. It states what could
and could not be verified by the cutoff without collecting or deriving any
private key, seed, or wallet material.
