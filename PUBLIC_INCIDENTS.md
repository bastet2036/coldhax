# Public incident accounting

Research cutoff: **2026-08-04 22:35:51 UTC**. On-chain balances below were
queried from the public [mempool.space API](https://mempool.space/api) at that
same time and include confirmed plus mempool funded outputs minus spent outputs.
The machine-readable observations and exact classifications are in
[`evidence/public-incidents.json`](evidence/public-incidents.json).

## Bottom line

Galaxy Research's newest high-confidence headline is **1,596 BTC across
approximately 7,300 source addresses**, combining three victim-confirmed major
waves and **14 smaller incidents**. Galaxy says **73 individual victims** have
contacted its analyst and that victim reports confirmed Waves 1–3 and helped
identify the smaller footprints. This supersedes the earlier 1,367.05 BTC /
4,585-address cumulative snapshot; the two figures must not be added.

This is not a vendor loss figure or a public cryptographic proof that every
source address was generated by the defective path. The complete address and
transaction corpus is not published, 73 people is not an address or incident
count, and Galaxy says the internally consistent waves need not share one
operator. Defect attribution therefore remains suspected, albeit now with
material victim corroboration.

In a subsequent [August 4 thread](https://x.com/intangiblecoins/status/2084584185528746434),
Galaxy's Alex Thorn raised the count to **15 additional smaller footprints
(A–O)** and at least 15 apparent attackers. He said one new anonymous victim
report of **less than 1 BTC** led to Footprint O, a reported **12 BTC from 126
addresses**. No address/transaction list or revised cumulative headline was
published. This report therefore preserves those rounded figures separately
and does not add 12 BTC to the August 3 1,596 BTC total.

Public owner/witness reports provide stronger evidence that at least some
Coldcard-origin wallets were swept without owner authorization. One owner now
publicly reports an exact **18.25245043 BTC** loss across three Coldcard wallets,
but did not publish the source addresses or transaction IDs needed to verify or
place that amount within the aggregate waves. A second owner reports that **2
BTC** held under a Mk3-generated wallet was drained while he was away, also
without public source addresses or a transaction ID. No reviewed public
source reconstructed a victim seed and matched it to a drained address, and no
source named or established the identity of an attacker. Therefore this report
classifies the aggregate waves and individual cases as **suspected attribution
to this defect**, not conclusively attributed exploitation.

Seven reviewed public Chainabuse owner reports include transaction IDs. Four
newly reviewed Mk3 reports bind exact source values of **4.53915589 BTC**,
**0.32531190 BTC**, **0.10435085 BTC**, and **0.1001 BTC** to public
transactions. The 4.53915589 BTC output moves into a published Galaxy Wave 3
vault; the next two overlap Galaxy Wave 2; and the 0.1001 BTC receipt overlaps
the July 31 community consolidation. Earlier cases bind 5.39099821 BTC to Wave
3, 5.13591373 BTC to 23 inputs inside the August 2 consolidation, and
0.17998515 BTC to three receipts in the P2TR sample. These are the strongest
public owner-to-transaction bindings in this review, but they still do not
cryptographically prove seed reconstruction. None is added again to an
overlapping cluster or cumulative headline.

Galaxy's Alex Thorn also reported a separate **nearly 30 BTC Coldcard victim**,
with approximately **17 BTC** peeled through THORChain into ETH and then sent to
Duel.com. The reviewed post supplies no victim/attacker address or BTC/ETH
transaction ID. This report preserves it as an approximate analyst-reported case
but excludes it from all totals because the route and distinctness cannot be
independently verified.

An August 3 fourth-wave alert has now been revised from its initial in-progress
figures to a **709-address / 448.73 BTC surviving core after excluding 89
multisig addresses / 20.58 BTC**. The analyst explicitly says there is still no
direct victim report confirming this wave. Galaxy's later thread calls it
medium-high confidence and reports 2,055 BTC if it is included, but still omits
it from the 1,596 BTC high-confidence headline. The complete corpus and an exact
Wave 4-only amount remain unpublished.

## Classification rules

These are separate questions:

1. **Affected/exposed:** Was the wallet generated on an affected device and
   firmware path?
2. **Unauthorized spend:** Did an owner identify a spend as unauthorized and
   provide enough public on-chain detail to inspect it?
3. **Attributed exploitation:** Does evidence establish that this exact entropy
   defect, rather than a migration, backup compromise, phishing, malware, or
   another cause, produced the spend?

A destination's current balance is neither the amount stolen nor a victim's
loss. Consolidation fees reduce destination balances. Later hops are not new
losses. Cumulative rows below must not be added together.

## Potential Wave 4 — not included in the verified cumulative total

At 2026-08-03 00:51 UTC, Galaxy's Alex Thorn
[reported a *likely* fourth wave](https://x.com/intangiblecoins/status/2084079706320646300)
then still in progress: 218 confirmed transactions, 462 source addresses, 216
fresh destinations, and 388.92748828 BTC in blocks 960778–960792. The blocks and
transactions exist, but this review did not independently reproduce the entire
address-set classifier or establish authorization for every spend. It is
therefore preserved as a **potential wave** and is **not added** to Galaxy's
high-confidence cumulative total.

At 03:22 UTC Thorn
[published a correction](https://x.com/intangiblecoins/status/2084117621864046698)
after Nunchuk identified multisig transactions in the confirmed-plus-mempool
pastebins. The update states: 857 addresses / 486.11 BTC as circulated; remove
89 addresses / 20.58 BTC for multisig; surviving core 709 addresses / 448.73
BTC. Those address counts and amounts do not arithmetically reconcile by simple
subtraction, so this report preserves the stated surviving-core values rather
than inventing a derivation. Thorn also states that Waves 1–3 contain zero
multisigs and that no direct victim report had yet confirmed Wave 4.

The community tracker separately subtracts six destinations with prior history
and publishes a derived 703-address / 443.34 BTC figure. Because that step
assumes those six do not overlap the 89 multisig exclusions, it is recorded as
a secondary reconstruction rather than replacing Thorn's primary stated core.

Galaxy's later [high-confidence update](https://x.com/glxyresearch/status/2084411904924045370)
still excludes Wave 4. Its
[thread](https://x.com/glxyresearch/status/2084411915745595527) calls the wave
medium-high confidence and says including it would produce **2,055 BTC**, but
still reports no specific victim confirmation. The thread does not publish an
exact Wave 4-only amount or complete corpus. This review therefore does not
derive one by subtracting 1,596 BTC, particularly because that result would not
exactly match the earlier stated 448.73 BTC core.
